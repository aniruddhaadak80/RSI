# Contributing To RSI Atlas

Thank you for reading this guide with care. This project grows through small, well tested changes and calm review. The notes below explain how to propose work that fits the loop.

## Design Ethos In Paragraphs

Prefer narrow patches over broad rewrites. A good change does one thing and proves it with a test. Prefer plain words over clever terms. A good doc paragraph can be read aloud without pause. Prefer grounded proof over praise. A good claim links to a test log, a score file, or a diary entry. Prefer separate judges over shared context. A good evaluation keeps the worker and the judge apart.

## Ways To Help In Paragraphs

Docs help is welcome in the form of clearer explanations, corrected tables, better diagrams, and new guided paths. Engine help is welcome in the form of small fixtures, tighter gates, clearer residue records, and cost views. Research help is welcome in the form of missing paper notes, corrected years, and better summaries. Safety help is welcome in the form of sharper threat notes, better sandbox defaults, and clearer rollback steps.

## Proposal Flow In Paragraphs

Phase One is conversation. Open an issue that names the problem, the narrow scope, and the expected proof. Share links to relevant papers or repo runs when they exist. Wait for a short design nod before building large work.

Phase Two is a branch. Create a focused branch from the latest mainline. Keep the diff small enough for one review sitting. Add or update a test with every behavior change. Update docs paragraphs when behavior or safety rules shift.

Phase Three is review. Open a pull request with a plain summary, a test report, a cost note when runtime changes, and a safety note when gates or frozen paths are touched. Respond to review notes with small follow up commits. Keep the tone steady and factual.

## Quality Bar In Paragraphs

Every promotion needs a full local test pass. Every new fixture needs a hidden check that the generator never sees during search. Every judge change needs calibration notes with known good and known bad cases. Every cost change needs before and after numbers. Every high risk change needs a human reviewer and a rollback note.

## Style Notes In Paragraphs

Write in paragraphs with generous spacing. Use tables for comparison. Use headings for rhythm. Use code blocks for exact commands and logic. Keep color calm with slate, emerald, teal, amber, cyan, and warm neutrals. Keep language simple so students and builders can share the same page.

## Code Of Conduct In Paragraphs

Treat all reviewers with patience. Assume good intent while asking for proof. Share credit with paper authors and repo maintainers. Mark uncertain claims as early stage. Correct mistakes quickly and kindly. The goal is a durable public record of grounded self improvement.
